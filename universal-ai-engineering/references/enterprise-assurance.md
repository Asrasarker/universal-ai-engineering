# Enterprise engineering assurance

Use the controls that match the system's real risk, customer commitments, and organizational policies. Start with what could fail, who or what would be affected, and how quickly the failure must be detected and reversed. Give each material control a testable requirement, owner, and evidence. Ask for specialist review where the project's policy or risk requires expertise or decision rights the agent lacks.

## Design and verification

- **Reliability and scale.** Identify critical journeys and define measurable service-level objectives with their indicators, workload, and measurement window. For capacity, record expected traffic and growth, latency targets, concurrency, dependency quotas, and cost per meaningful unit where applicable. Exercise representative load, degraded dependencies, resource exhaustion, and recovery in an authorized isolated environment. Tie alerting and rollout stops to user impact or exhausted error budget. Validate results against agreed targets. [Google SRE guidance on SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/)
- **Recovery and migration.** Agree how much downtime and data loss are tolerable. Rehearse restoration against recovery time and recovery point objectives when applicable. For migrations, verify compatibility with old clients and records, reconciled counts or totals, resumption after interruption, cutover ordering, and rollback or forward recovery. Identify the fallback owner and decision deadline. [AWS Well-Architected reliability guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/reliability-pillar/)
- **Identity and data governance.** Map trust boundaries, sensitive data flows, and entry points. When applicable, verify tenant isolation across APIs, jobs, caches, exports, and storage using test accounts and fixtures. Review least privilege, role and service account lifecycle, enterprise sign-in and deprovisioning requirements, administrative action audit trails, data handling and disposal, and third-party access. Identify which customer, organizational, or regulatory obligations actually apply and route formal judgments to the accountable reviewer. [NIST Secure Software Development Framework](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final)
- **AI governance.** For material AI risk, record intended use, affected users, accountable owner, provider data handling, prohibited or high-risk actions, and human escalation and fallback. Version the model and evaluation set, compare with a baseline, measure regressions by relevant user or task segment, and define triggers to pause, roll back, or reevaluate. Record model or provider changes as reasons to rerun applicable checks. [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)

## Evidence that follows a release

- Inspect actual CI results and required review or approval records for the candidate. Check which branch, commit, artifact, configuration, and environment the evidence covers. Verify that release promotion uses the tested artifact where supported, or gather replacement evidence for material differences. Identify missing evidence as a gap.
- When implementing delivery controls is in scope, put repeatable required checks in the existing CI or release workflow. Verify that a failed required check prevents promotion in an authorized test setting. Keep business sign-off and any required specialist decisions with their designated owners.
- Where required, preserve a software bill of materials, dependency and license review, and artifact signing or build provenance. Apply supply-chain checks through the project's supported CI and release workflow. Retain evidence in an access-controlled location according to existing policy. [SLSA build track basics](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/build-track-basics)
- Review findings by likelihood and impact. Record material risks, mitigations, evidence, and the accountable owner. Document authorized, policy-permitted exceptions with scope, justification, compensating controls, and an expiry or review date. Use the main skill's release decision rules for unresolved findings or missing evidence.

## Operate and improve

- Before handoff, verify that responders can see alerts, access runbooks, and take the agreed containment or recovery actions. For critical services, rehearse a realistic incident and capture what the team actually observed, where escalation failed, and which fixes have an owner. After an incident, track corrective actions and verify the change that prevents recurrence.
- For delivery improvement, baseline useful service-level measures from observed data. DORA measures include change lead time, deployment frequency, failed deployment recovery time, change fail rate, and deployment rework rate. Pick measures that illuminate a bottleneck, compare the same service over time, and keep them distinct from customer adoption and production reliability. [DORA software delivery performance metrics](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/)
